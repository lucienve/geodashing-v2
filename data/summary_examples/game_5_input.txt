Back on the grid! Indeed after mostly recovering from shoulder surgery (although I'm still in physical therapy), and now that I had a lengthy and uneventful Saturday planned, I was finally able to get out to this VERY local dashpoint. GD005-BIWG lies on the west side of Dover, NH (we live on the northwest side in a little "suburb" of Somersworth). 

The actual point of GD005-BIWG is about 17 meters southwest of the corner of Columbus Avenue and Lucy Lane - a short little road that meanders back into a subdivision around 20 years old consisting of 4 bedroom homes around 2500 square feet (232 square meters). The Mrs. and I were able to drive up on Columbus and get a reading on the Geodashing site of being within 3 meters.

I took two photos - one northeast of the point and the stop sign on Lucy Lane pointing toward the point, and the other from about 27 meters southwest of the dashpoint - where the home had a rock wall. 

Seriously: New Hampshire is known as "The Granite State" for a reason - we have a LOT of rocks (almost as much cornfields as we had in Illinois). The people that settled this area just over 400 years ago moved a LOT of these large rocks from their farm fields to make boundary walls for property. The base stones of these rock walls were known as "Two-Man" rocks, as they weighed between 90 and 213 KG, the "Medium" or "Stacking Stones" weigh anywhere from 23 to 90 KG, and the "Capstones" weigh from 34-63 KG. These were stronger and sturdier folks than I.

3 points for Markwell and the Mrs.

I headed out on a quiet, rainy late-summer afternoon into the aptly named town of 'Lumberland' in Sullivan County, just on the New York side of the Delaware river.

I didn't make it to the dashpoint itself, which was down a chained-closed unmarked gravel road, though it is shown as the public 'Elle Road' on Google maps. There is a realtor's sign, presumably for a lot in the Lake Diana subdivision, where individual wooded parcels were platted out long ago, but don't seem to ever have been developed.

The road I was on, 'Taras Shevchenko Road', is named after 'the founder of modern Ukrainian literature' (or so Google tells me), and this seems to be somewhat of an enclave - there's two ornate traditional wooden churches not far away, and many other road names named after other prominent Ukrainians.
  On my return route, I stopped along NY Route 97 at the Hawk's Nest overlook, a winding cliffside road above the Delaware River that has served as a backdrop for car commercials and movies (Benedict Cumberbatch crashes his Lamborghini here in Doctor Strange).  Today, it was just a few hardy souls out in kayaks, down in the river at the bottom.
