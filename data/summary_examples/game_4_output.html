Game GD004 (The Ocean is Inconveniently Large) of Geodashing was won by mapreader4, who secured a victory with 14 points (in their first game!)

Tied for second place are DEEDEEDASH and douq millar, each scoring a solid 9 points. Other players scoring points this month include Ash Doge (3 points), lucienve (3 points), MsAdventure (3 points), and rhomicron (3 points).

The game featured 15 successful hunts, all of them taking place across the varied landscapes of the United States.

A sampling of waypoints visited by Geodashing players this month:

near Norfolk, Nebraska, at a scenic state recreation area with a pretty lake where the point itself lay near an RV waste dump station ("not the prettiest spot of this area").

near Columbus, Nebraska, in a tall green cornfield near the remarkably prosperous town of Lindsay, home to a global irrigation manufacturing plant.

near Grand Island, Nebraska, in a vibrant green cornfield along a busy major highway where modern navigation is "much more prone to error."

near Gainesville, Georgia, down Old Cornelia Highway near a local family honey farm and the quirky, unincorporated community of Rabbittown.

near Northglenn, Colorado, in an empty urban lot flanked by a modern car wash on one side and Westminster suburban developments on the other.

near Granger, Indiana, in a commercial strip parking lot near a "Duck Crossing" sign ("not something you see too often").

near Addison, Illinois, next to a municipal pumping station on the way to pick up a friend from the airport.

near Buffalo Grove, Illinois, near a local church, scored from across the street to avoid trespassing on private property.

near Bourbonnais, Illinois, under massive cross-country power transmission lines slicing through beautiful, sweeping rural scenery.

near Kalamazoo, Michigan, in a quiet residential area, which prompted a lovely visit to a local bookstore cafe.

near Knoxville, Tennessee, in a commercial parking lot bordered by a tree-lined street and a black bench.

About Geodashing: Geodashing is a game in which players use GPS receivers on a playing field that covers the entire planet. The waypoints, or dashpoints, to be reached are randomly selected. The win goes to who can get to the most dashpoints; that is, if you can get to them at all! Each game has a new set of dashpoints making each game different and unpredictable. For more information and to play, visit Geodashing.org.
