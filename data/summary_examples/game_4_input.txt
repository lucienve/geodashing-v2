On my way to see family in Georgia for the long weekend, this was an easy detour from my drive south on US-23/Cornelia Highway, to a dashpoint on Old Cornelia Highway, so there is some implied history there. Nighttime, so not too much to see; I took this photo of the nearby football stadium entrance. Not 150 meters from the dashpoint was the honey-producing McGraw Family Farm, which amused me since I went to a Tennessee dashpoint in April that was also by a honey farm. 

On the way back to the modern highway I passed through unincorporated Rabbittown, a name I liked. Apparently in the 1900s, locals trapped and raised rabbits to supplement their diets. Turns out there is also a 20-foot rabbit statue which was made in somewhat comedic response to nearby (much larger) Gainesville's (much larger) chicken statue. Wish I'd known, I'll have to come back in the daylight for that one.

GD004-ALKW sits in a vacant lot northeast of the intersection of W 96th Ave and Federal Blvd in the Denver metro area. Since I was already within the scoring circle as I drove along the 3 streets surrounding the dashpoint, I didn’t bother to wade through the grass and weeds to zero. 

This dashpoint’s location is really a tale of two cities. On the east side of Federal Blvd, Federal Heights is more urban. A car wash, gas station, convenience store, fast food restaurant, and self-storage units surrounded the dashpoint. On the west side of Federal Blvd, Westminster is more suburban, with housing developments and a Catholic Church. Instead of Big Ben, I could see twin water towers in the background.
