Game GD002 (The Herd Finds Its Legs) of Geodashing was won by DEEDEEDASH and douq millar, who tied for the top spot with a spectacular 18 points each!

Individual honors also go to Wisk with 6 points. Honorable mentions go to Ash Doge, dewi o dre-gwyr, Pittus, and rhomicron, who each secured 3 points this round. lucienve also managed a few attempts, though not scoring any points.

The game saw 14 unique dashpoints successfully visited across four countries (the United States, Germany, the United Kingdom, and Canada).

A sampling of waypoints visited by Geodashing players this month:

on a flat summit above Central City, Colorado, where narrow, winding roads past local casinos led to a hidden outhouse structure nestled behind a fence.

in a lush, landscaped garden near Luedenscheid, Germany, where a beautifully pruned red Japanese maple stood in vibrant contrast to green spring foliage.

near a dry creek bed outside the tiny town of Leshara, Nebraska, where players navigated dirt roads to find an unplanted cornfield during a dry spell.

in a bare field near Clarkson, Nebraska, where parched terrain and nearby wind turbines highlighted the area's current drought conditions.

along a scenic driving route near Carmarthen, Wales, easily picked up on a road trip heading toward Newport Pembs.

at a rural intersection near Ann Arbor, Michigan, marked by a massive, spreading oak tree and a classic weathered white barn.

outside a fenced swimming pool in Dublin, Ohio, where the point was scored twice in the middle of a heavy, pouring rainstorm.

high above George Inlet near Ketchikan, Alaska, where players bypassed a surface approach to fly in a rugged 1961 de Havilland Beaver floatplane and caught sight of a pod of three orcas swimming along the inside passage.

along the uniquely named Six and Twenty Road near Pendleton, South Carolina, where a friendly bunch of cows and donkeys watched from a grassy roadside pasture.

at a school drop-off zone in suburban Des Moines, Iowa, where kids were busy celebrating a sunny, end-of-school field day near a towering municipal water tower.

deep within a heavily wooded, rolling landscape south of Des Moines, Iowa, which required backtracking down gravel roads after a fun detour through the city.

near a quiet alpaca farm outside Council Bluffs, Iowa, where vibrant spring green fields lined the route back to the highway.

on a quiet section of the Appalachian Trail in Warwick, New York, where dense undergrowth of knee-high briars made for a challenging final stretch, but a bulletin board featured a note thanking a local "trail angel (not Angle)" with love.

About Geodashing: Geodashing is a game in which players use GPS receivers on a playing field that covers the entire planet. The waypoints, or dashpoints, to be reached are randomly selected. The win goes to who can get to the most dashpoints; that is, if you can get to them at all! Each game has a new set of dashpoints making each game different and unpredictable. For more information and to play, visit Geodashing.org.
