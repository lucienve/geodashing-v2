Macadamia farms are being cleared frequently around here to make way for more profitable crops. They were once a good way to make money, but local macadamias can't compete with overseas ones, so local farmers are pushing them out to make way for more profitable things.

First Dash of the new game, and results were the same as my first-first
20.21 miles from home

Dashpoint Name: Wakeda Campground is Closed

It's a new dawn for Geodashing, new areas to explore after moving for the second time in 5 years (to New Hampshire) and new llamas to meet. My youngest Markwell offspring first went with me on a Geodashing adventure when he was just under the age of 2 in September of 2001 in Coal City, Illinois. During the first iteration of the game, he accompanied me on 208 out of the 455 hunts I attempted (just under half). The two of us piled into the car with Howie, our newest corgi and headed out the 20.2 miles (32.52 KM) almost due south to hit what I thought would be a drive-by. Along the way, we saw many of the typical sites of New Hampshire, which is quite a change from the flat plains of Illinois: rolling hills, winding back roads and rock walls. LOTS of rock walls.

From my recon, I knew the dashpoint would be in Wakeda Campground just south of Exeter, NH, and should be reachable by car, but as we turned on to "Main Road" (which was evidently so named as the "main road of the campground", not your typical "Main Street"), we were greeted with an insurmountable barrier. Signs stating "Stop. Do not enter. Work in Progress. No Walkers, No Visitors. Campers call ***-***-**** prior to entry." We were stymied a full 1,383 meters from our goal due to bad timing.

So I guess this just echos my luck of my first attempted dashpoint in June of 2001. Twenty-five years later, and I still didn't get my first point.

Wakeda Campground
https://www.wakedacampground.com/

Dashpoint Name: Road Side Rock Walls and the Mall of New Hampshire

My wonderful wife decided that she wants to see more of the state as well, so on a Sunday afternoon we piled into the Honda CRV with Howie the Wonder Dog and drove out exactly 50 KM southwest to Manchester, NH. After the relaxing drive along the very familiar Route 101 that spans the East-West path of the state, we exited the large arterial road at the entrance to the "Mall of New Hampshire". To believe the Google Reviews, the mall is starting to wane in effectiveness - something I've seen repeatedly in many locations since the pandemic. There are more and more empty store fronts and very few people in the malls themselves.

The dashpoint itself is an easy drive down a country road that skirts between the two halves of Massabesic Lake. Once within the scoring range, we continued south of the point and turned around at an eastern facing entrance to a horse farm (stopping to take a photo of the entrance), and then back northbound, stopping near a driveway about 37 meters north-north-east of the point. From there I exited the car and crossed the street taking a picture of the remnants of a barn - just the stone foundation, and then one of the house across the street (photos included). Definitely within the scoring range this time!
